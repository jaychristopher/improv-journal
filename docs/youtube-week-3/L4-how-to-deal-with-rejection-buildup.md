## SOP 01 · Persona + JTBD

### Persona brainstorm (6)

1. **Recently dumped 30-something** processing romantic breakup pain
2. **Job seeker** in a soft market hitting application rejections weekly
3. **Aspiring creator/entrepreneur** whose pitch / submission / book proposal got rejected
4. **Person ghosted or excluded** from a friend group or social circle
5. **Sales / BD professional** hitting cold-outreach rejection daily
6. **Performer / actor / musician** facing audition cycles

### Primary persona

**The chronic-exposure rejection-taker** — job seeker in month 2-4 of search, OR salesperson / BD pro running outbound, OR creator pitching their work. Specifically: someone who is *currently* and *repeatedly* hitting rejection, not someone processing a single-event wound.

**Why this slice over romantic / single-event:**

- Search-volume: romantic rejection is bigger raw, but the SERP is saturated with grief-frame content
- **Channel POV alignment:** improv's value-add is exposure calibration — it's strongest for people who keep getting back up to bat. Single-event grief needs a therapist, not an improv frame
- Returning-need vs one-time crisis: chronic-rejection viewer is more likely to subscribe; breakup-grief viewer is one-and-done
- Bridge content's three practices — fail faster, move immediately, separate event from meaning — all assume volume. They don't fit a one-shot heartbreak

### JTBD — 4 lenses

- **Functional:** Recover faster between rejections so I can take the next swing without a multi-day stall
- **Emotional:** Feel like I'm someone who handles failure, not someone who falls apart; restore my agency in the loop
- **Social:** Be seen as resilient and gritty by people watching me try, not as fragile or pitiable
- **Anti-job:** No toxic positivity ("everything happens for a reason"). No 20-minute self-help lecture. No "just stop caring." No advice that reads as "you weren't trying hard enough." I want to get back in the arena, not feel better in a recliner

### Persona expectations

- **Length:** 4-6 min (not a 20-min lecture)
- **Format:** principle + practice — one frame, three reps
- **Tone:** direct, not coddling; acknowledges the pain without dwelling
- **Examples vs theory:** heavy on practice (specific reps), light on theory (just enough fMRI to ground it)
- **Time investment:** watch once, apply this week

### SERP white-space angle

**Top SERP for "how to deal with rejection" (pre-search inferred from prior cycles + standard SERP patterns):**

- Generic self-help: "don't take it personally" / "give yourself time" / "remember your worth"
- Therapist-Q&A articles: "validate your feelings" / "talk to someone"
- Mel Robbins-adjacent: "rejection is redirection"
- Religious / spiritual: "God's plan" / "everything happens for a reason"

**What no one is saying:**

> **Rejection isn't the problem. The freeze AFTER rejection is.**

The pain is real — fMRI confirms it. But the suffering happens in the gap between "they said no" and your next action. That gap is where rumination happens, where the event becomes an identity, where the threat system miscalibrates. Improv eliminates the gap by design — the next scene starts in 30 seconds, you literally cannot ruminate. That structural property is what civilians can borrow.

This is the channel-native angle: not "feel better about rejection," but "shorten the gap between rejection and your next attempt — and the rest takes care of itself."

### Three anchor insights (each cited)

**1. Rejection activates the same neural circuit as physical pain.**
- Source: Eisenberger, Lieberman, & Williams, *Science* (2003) — "Does Rejection Hurt? An fMRI Study of Social Exclusion." Anterior cingulate cortex lights up in both.
- Why load-bearing: validates the pain as real (not weakness). Defangs "just get over it." Establishes the channel's evidence-based register.

**2. Del Close: "Fall, then figure out what to do on the way down."**
- Source: Del Close (1934-1999); principle codified in *Truth in Comedy* by Halpern, Close & Johnson (1994). The Second City / iO Theater foundational text.
- Why load-bearing: the channel's quotable centerpiece. Gives the video its callback. Connects rejection-handling directly to improv lineage.

**3. The gap is where suffering concentrates — improv removes the gap by design.**
- Source: practitioner synthesis grounded in prolonged-exposure-therapy literature (Foa, Hembree & Rothbaum, *Prolonged Exposure Therapy for PTSD*, 2007). Improv as a real-world calibration laboratory.
- Why load-bearing: this is the white-space angle made operational. Gives the viewer the specific behavior to copy ("close the gap").
