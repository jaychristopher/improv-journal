# PG-2 — Longform opens on one word and the generator has none

Classes and shortform ask for what the seven kinds already give: who, where
and what; a first line; a task; something already wrong; a question for the
crowd. Longform asks for one word — the Harold's suggestion, the word an
Armando monologist takes — and the generator has nothing for it, because the
guide argues, rightly, that a random noun is the weakest start for a
two-person scene.

The resolution is the argument itself: a longform opening is the one place a
single word is the right ask, because the scenes come from three minutes of
association and not from the word, and a good word for that is a common noun
with a smell, a weight or a history, not "banana". So: about eighty curated
words in their own small bank, outside the 487 so the title stays honest, a
guide section that makes the case and lists every word, and a tile in the
hero beside the classic. The owner's teacher reads the draft first; nothing
ships before that.

Not on the board: Andi Smith's other buckets — jobs, objects, emotions,
genres, famous people, songs. They are game fodder, not starts, and Ahrefs
(US, 2026-09-30) has nothing behind any of them.
