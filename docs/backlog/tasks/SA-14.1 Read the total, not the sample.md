# SA-14.1 — Read the total, not the sample

## What was found

Ahrefs `gsc-performance-history`, project 9723388, monthly:

| Month   | Clicks | Impressions | CTR       | Avg position |
| ------- | ------ | ----------- | --------- | ------------ |
| 2026-04 | 0      | 45          | 0%        | 60.8         |
| 2026-05 | 3      | 1,108       | 0.27%     | 9.4          |
| 2026-06 | 2      | **4,539**   | 0.04%     | **5.5**      |
| 2026-07 | 12     | 820         | 1.46%     | 9.1          |
| 2026-08 | **19** | 1,243       | **1.53%** | 21.8         |

**7,755 impressions and 36 clicks since April.** For the 2026-06-01 → 2026-09-25
window this backlog has been quoting: **6,602 impressions and 33 clicks**.

The per-query and per-page tables for that same window return **13 queries, 12
pages, 35 impressions and 0 clicks** — 0.5% of the impressions and none of the
clicks. Search Console withholds queries below a privacy threshold; those tables
show only what clears it.

Thirteen firings of this audit treated the visible 35 as the whole. SA-10.1 said
so in as many words — "the complete measured demand this site has ever
captured" — and that sentence is wrong by two orders of magnitude. It has been
corrected in place, as has SA-1.1's framing.

**The withheld portion cannot be recovered from either instrument.**
`gsc-anonymous-queries` exists for exactly this and returns empty for this
project: it resolves withheld queries against Ahrefs' keyword index, and
SA-10.1 established that this site's vocabulary sits below that index's floor.
Two unrelated blindnesses, the same 99.5%.

## What the trend actually says

Worth reading carefully, because it is the first trajectory anybody has looked
at and it is not the shape the snapshot implied.

- **Clicks are rising**: 0, 3, 2, 12, 19. August is the best month on record.
- **CTR is rising sharply**: 0.04% in June to 1.53% in August, a 35× move.
- **Average position is worsening**: 5.5 in June to 21.8 in August.
- **June is an outlier**: 4,539 impressions at position 5.5 and two clicks.

Position worsening while clicks and CTR rise is the signature of _breadth_ — more
pages surfacing on more terms, most of them further down, rather than fewer pages
ranking better. That is exactly what a long-tail atom corpus should look like as
it is indexed, and it means average position is the wrong headline metric for
this site. It will keep getting worse as the strategy works.

**September is missing** from the connector's response entirely. Do not read that
as a collapse; `seo-audit.mjs` already records that this connector's bucketing
is unreliable at narrow ranges.

## Run

1. **Correct the remaining cards.** SA-10.1 and SA-1.1 are done. Check SA-4.1,
   SA-5.1, SA-6.1, SA-1.2 and SA-1.3 for any claim that rests on "12 pages" or
   "13 queries" being the site's whole search presence. The findings mostly
   survive — they are about the identified pages — but any sentence that
   generalises from the sample to the site needs the qualifier.
2. **Put both numbers in the audit, side by side.** `seo-audit.mjs` carries a
   dated GSC block holding the page-level reading. Add the aggregate next to it
   with the sample's share stated as a percentage, so the next reader cannot
   mistake one for the other. That is the durable half of this card.
3. **Stop reporting average position as the headline.** Record clicks, CTR and
   impressions as the trend metrics, and keep position only as a per-page
   control the way SA-1.1 uses it. A corpus that wins by breadth has a position
   average that degrades as it succeeds, and a metric that moves the wrong way
   when things go right will eventually be acted on.
4. **Re-read the trend, not the snapshot, from now on.** Every card that
   schedules a 30-day re-read — SA-1.1, SA-4.1, SA-6.1, SA-7.1 — should read
   `gsc-performance-history` as well as `gsc-pages`, or it will measure the
   0.5% again.
