### Iter 1 (this turn) — persona + JTBD draft

**Personas who type "how to be funny":**
1. **Socially anxious 20-something** — wants to be more likeable, freezes in groups. Believes funny = confidence.
2. **Dating / new-relationships seeker** — Tinder, first dates, "I'm bad at small talk."
3. **Professional networker / new hire** — wants to be memorable in meetings, presentations, Slack.
4. **Aspiring creator / streamer** — needs personality on camera, fears coming across stiff.
5. **Student / teen** — peer status, popularity, group identity.
6. **Parent / teacher** — wants to hold attention with kids/students.

**Best-fit primary persona:** the **socially anxious 20-something**. Largest search volume slice, strongest emotional hook, highest mass-market resonance. Other personas pull from the same content.

**Jobs to be done:**
- **Functional:** "Make people laugh at things I say without sounding like I'm trying."
- **Emotional:** "Feel like the person others want around, not the boring one."
- **Social:** "Become the kind of person who's invited to things."
- **Anti-job:** "Don't make me memorize jokes or watch standup."

**What the persona expects when they search:**
- Practical tips usable in next conversation
- Permission to NOT be a comedian
- Specific moves, not theory
- Quick wins (3-5 min video, not 20)
- Reassurance that funny is learnable

**Improv-perspective white space (the differentiator):**
- "Funny isn't about jokes. It's about noticing what's already absurd."
- **Mick Napier (Annoyance Theatre):** "The obvious choice is always funnier than the clever one." The clever choice is what your editing brain produces; the obvious choice is what actually gets a laugh.
- **Keith Johnstone:** "Good actors are not afraid to look stupid." Funny people commit. Comedy dies on hesitation.
- **Del Close:** "Find the game of the scene." Once you know the pattern, you keep playing it. Funny isn't one joke — it's a pattern you build.
- **Anti-pattern:** "Yes And" misapplied → being agreeable on autopilot isn't funny.

**Three insights to anchor the video around:**
1. **The obvious choice is funnier than the clever one** (Napier) — counterintuitive payoff
2. **Commit fully or it dies** (Johnstone) — explains why "trying to be funny" fails
3. **Find the pattern, repeat it** (Close) — turns one laugh into a scene

**Script working-title candidates:**
- "How to Be Funny: The Improv Secret to Quick Wit"
- "How to Be Funny Without Telling Jokes"
- "How to Be Funny: 3 Rules from 60 Years of Improv"

**Open questions for next iter:**

**Bridge thesis (already articulated):**
1. **Stop trying to be funny.** Del Close: "One of the biggest mistakes an improviser can make is attempting to be funny." Truth in Comedy (1994).
2. **Honesty — not jokes.** The unexpectedly true beat outperforms the prepared joke.
3. **Specificity — not exaggeration.** "My boss starts every Zoom by saying 'Can everyone see my screen?' and waiting 30 seconds for confirmation from each individual person."
4. **Pattern recognition — not joke construction.** Notice the weird thing, follow it.
5. **Shared discovery — not performance.** Funny WITH someone, not funny AT someone.

**Sources to cite (already in bridge):**
- Close, Halpern, Johnson (1994), _Truth in Comedy_
- Johnstone (1979), _Impro_
- Besser, Roberts, Walsh (2013), _UCB Comedy Improvisation Manual_
- Limb & Braun (2008), _PLoS ONE_ — fMRI study on jazz improvisation showing self-monitoring deactivates during spontaneous creation
- Hurley, Dennett, Adams (2011), _Inside Jokes_, MIT Press

**Differentiator confirmed:** the script can lead with "stop trying to be funny" as a pattern interrupt against every other top result, then deliver the bandwidth/honesty reframe with named teachers and the Limb fMRI data — citation magnetism (just like L31).

### 5 highest-ROI edits for v2

1. **Add explicit stakes in first 15s.** After the hook, before "every article" paragraph: "And every minute you spend trying — the real funny moment was already happening, and you missed it." Names the cost.

2. **Move the "three moves" tease to first 30s.** Currently at 1:30 (after the fMRI). Pull it forward: end the hook section with "Three moves that produce humor — none of which involve trying. I'll show you all three in four minutes." Earlier promise = stronger retention pull.

3. **Re-anchor mid-point on the Johnstone paradox.** Currently at ~2:00-2:15 we're mid-Move-1. Restructure so that the "the more obvious an improviser is, the more original he appears" quote LANDS at exactly the midpoint, with a beat of silence around it. That's the most quotable line in the entire script and it deserves the audio anchor weight.

4. **Test the leaner hook.** "Stop trying. Start noticing." (5 words, 2 seconds) versus current "If you want to be funny, stop trying to be funny." (9 words, 3.5s). Leaner version is more punchable, matches L1's pattern of opening on the action verb. Trade-off: explicit "be funny" SEO ping is in the longer version. **Decision pending — propose hybrid:** "Stop trying. Start noticing. That's how you actually become funny." 12 words, captures both.

5. **Tighten Move 3 (pattern recognition).** Concept density is heaviest here — 90 words covering apology escalation + meta-explanation. Cut to ~60 words by trimming the "good news for analytical brains" beat into the closing paragraph instead.
