## 1 — immediate — "Six notes, starting with the one nobody sends first"

You asked for the six notes. Here they are, one every few days, and then
this stops — there is no newsletter behind it.

Before the first one, the thing most lists get wrong: a prompt, a game and
a question are three different objects, and reaching for the wrong one is
the most common way an evening goes flat.

- A **prompt** starts a scene. It needs to be specific enough to act on and
  open enough to be spoiled by a plan.
- A **game** has rules an audience can follow and a comic engine of its own.
- An **exercise** isolates one skill and has a failure you can coach. It is
  usually not much fun to watch — which is fine, because nobody is watching.

Those three lines are the site's own, from `GAME_JOBS` in the games picker.
The rest of this sequence is one note on each kind.

→ The generator: https://www.physicsofconnection.com/improv-prompts

## 2 — +2 days — "The first line is the whole scene"

Recycles: `content/atoms/first-line-drill.md`, `content/atoms/blind-offer.md`

Most scenes are decided before anybody has said anything true. The fix is
not a better idea; it is a smaller one.

**First Line Drill.** Players line up. One at a time, each steps out and
delivers a single declarative first line that commits to *something* — a
feeling, a relationship, a situation. Don't try to establish everything;
one specific commitment is enough. No response. No scene. Just the first
line, then sit down. Run it rapid-fire, two or three rounds minimum, so
everybody gets past their first attempt. *(Quoted from the atom's Setup.)*

What it trains is the threshold moment of starting — and the reason for the
rounds is that the first attempt is always the planned one.

→ https://www.physicsofconnection.com/practice/exercises/first-line-drill

## 3 — +5 days — "When nobody will start"

Recycles: `SYMPTOMS` in `src/lib/game-picker.ts` — this email is the page's
own diagnosis list, verbatim.

The usual way to pick a game is to find one that sounds fun. The better way
is to name what is going wrong and pick the game that isolates it. Six
things go wrong, and each has its own answer:

| What you are seeing | What to run |
|---|---|
| Nobody is listening | Mirroring, Pass the Clap |
| Everyone is planning | Zip Zap Zop, Big Booty |
| Scenes are clever but cold | Emotional Honesty Scene, Gift Giving |
| Nobody will start | First Line Drill, Blind Offer |
| Scenes come apart halfway through | Fracture Repair Drill |
| Everyone talks at once | Group Mind Cultivation |

That table is the site's, not a list assembled for this email — the picker
on the games page deals from exactly it.

→ https://www.physicsofconnection.com/improv-games

## 4 — +9 days — "Receive before you respond"

Recycles: `content/atoms/accepting-the-offer.md`

"Yes, and" is the most quoted rule in improv and the least useful as
stated, because it describes the output and not the move.

The move underneath it is **accepting the offer**: the disciplined practice
of receiving incoming information and treating it as valid *before* you
evaluate it. *(Quoted from the atom.)* Three steps, in order — receive,
then accept, then build. Most failures are at step one, and they look like
disagreement when they are actually inattention.

→ https://www.physicsofconnection.com/practice/techniques/accepting-the-offer

## 5 — +14 days — "A warm-up is not a small exercise"

Recycles: `GAME_JOBS` in `src/lib/game-picker.ts`

The classic mistake is running an exercise as though it were a performance
game. The moment players sense an audience they start playing for the
laugh, and whatever the exercise was isolating is gone.

So before you pick, answer which of the three you need:

- **Warm the room up** — cheap, fast, no wrong answers. Two minutes, no notes.
- **Fix something** — an exercise isolates one skill and has a failure you
  can coach. Run it slowly and interrupt often.
- **Something to play** — rules an audience can follow, and a comic engine
  of its own.

→ https://www.physicsofconnection.com/improv-games
