# L1 Keyframe Variants — Critique Round

**Figma file:** https://www.figma.com/design/7sIjwdCjkyNHH2m7f1DS8a/Video
**Page:** 🧠 L1 Overthinking
**Layout:** rows = peaks (1-10 in script order), columns = variants (A diagrammatic / B typographic / C metaphoric)

## Critique framework

For each variant I rate three dimensions on a 1-5 scale and one binary verdict:

- **Pause-worthiness** — would a viewer screenshot this and have a self-contained idea? (5 = standalone Instagram post; 1 = nonsense out of context)
- **Citation-friendliness** — can AI image-recognition / accessibility tools describe this accurately? (5 = unambiguous structural description; 1 = abstract noise)
- **Distinctiveness** — does it feel different from the previous peak's frame? (5 = clearly new visual logic; 1 = "didn't I just see this?")
- **Verdict** — `KEEP`, `REFINE`, or `KILL` (the latter only if the variant is fundamentally broken)

The goal is to identify the **best base concept per peak**, then improve it in the final-version round.

## Peak 5 · Internal computation (the imbalance reveal)

### Variant A · chip + overloaded gauge + thick PLAN / starved RECV arrows
- Pause: 4 · Citation: 5 · Distinct: 3
- **Strengths:** Continuity with Peak 4 — viewer sees the SAME diagram now imbalanced. Cinematic: it's the diagram updating.
- **Weaknesses:** Looks too similar to Peak 4 at a glance — distinctiveness is low. The overload framing on the gauge could read as just "more red."
- **Verdict:** KEEP but exploit the continuity intentionally — explicitly mark this as "SAME DIAGRAM, ONE BEAT LATER" so the viewer locks onto the mutation.

### Variant B · word columns: planning overflow vs receiving empty
- Pause: 4 · Citation: 4 · Distinct: 5
- **Strengths:** Visual proof-by-density. Left column packed, right column nearly empty. The single "...what did they say?" lone phrase is poetic.
- **Weaknesses:** Risks looking like a busy infographic. The varied font sizes/positions of the planning words can feel chaotic — but that IS the concept.
- **Verdict:** REFINE — keep the chaos in the left column, but constrain the words to ~6 instead of 14 to reduce noise. Make the right column's lone phrase larger.

### Variant C · attention pie chart 95% / 5%
- Pause: 5 · Citation: 5 · Distinct: 5
- **Strengths:** Pie chart makes the imbalance unarguable. Numbers (95%, 5%) provide false-precision authority. Highly shareable as a stat-graphic.
- **Weaknesses:** "95%" is a fabricated number — viewers may pause on the citation question. The thin slice is positioned awkwardly at the top.
- **Verdict:** REFINE — either ground the percentages in research (cognitive load studies cite ~80/20 in similar contexts) or remove the percentages and use proportional language ("most" / "almost none"). Reposition slice for visual clarity.

**Best base for final: A** (continuity with Peak 4 anchor) OR **C** (if a separate single-frame stat-graphic is desired)
*Pick based on whether continuity matters more than novelty.*

## Cross-cutting refinements for the final round

These apply across all peaks regardless of variant:

1. **Tighten brand consistency.** Some frames use Inter (we don't have a true serif). Decide: commit to Inter throughout (sans-serif, modern) OR add a serif font (e.g., Source Serif) for headlines. Right now we're inconsistent in INTENT vs execution.
2. **Stick figures need an upgrade.** They currently look like quick sketches. Either commit to filled silhouettes (more polished) or remove them in favor of icon symbols.
3. **Watermark placement is consistent — keep it.**
4. **Variant tags (A/B/C corner badges)** should be removed in the final versions — they're scaffolding.
5. **Corner safe-area** — the watermark sometimes hits the same line as captions. Add a 64px guard around the bottom edge.
