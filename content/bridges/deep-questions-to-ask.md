# Deep Questions to Ask: 120 That Get an Honest Answer

A deep question that gets a shallow answer has not failed because the other person was guarded. It has usually failed because of how it was built.

"What is your greatest fear?" asks somebody to summarise themselves. Summaries are performances — the answer arrives pre-edited, because to produce it you had to review your whole life and select. "When were you last properly frightened?" asks for a memory instead, and memories come with detail, and detail is where the actual thing is.

That is the difference these 120 are built on: **ask for an experience, not a conclusion.**

## Why Most Deep Questions Get Shallow Answers

Three failure modes, and they account for nearly all of it.

**The question asks for a conclusion.** Greatest fear, biggest regret, proudest moment, what you value most. Each one requires a summary, and the summary is always tidier than the truth. Ask about an instance and you get the real thing by accident.

**The question is too large to enter.** "What is the meaning of life?" has no foothold. "What is something you have decided is worth your time?" is the same territory with a floor under it.

**The question arrives before it has been earned.** This is the big one, and it is the next section.

## Depth Is a Condition, Not a Question

You cannot make a conversation deep by choosing a deeper question. Depth is a property of the conditions, and the conditions are two things.

**Trust, which is cumulative.** `trust` on this site is an accumulated estimate — built from many small exchanges — of whether it is safe to be exposed around this particular person. It is not a switch you flip with a good question. Every honest answer that goes unpunished updates it slightly upward, and every one that gets judged, fixed or repeated to somebody else updates it down.

**Reciprocity, which has to start with you.** `vulnerability` is being seen without armour, and it is not something you can ask another person to supply on your behalf. A question that asks for more openness than you have offered reads as an interview, and people answer interviews carefully. Answer your own question — first, or immediately after, at the same depth.

The practical version of both: `safety-in-the-room` is a precondition rather than an outcome. Asking for depth in order to create closeness has the causation backwards.

## The Ladder

You cannot jump to the top. What you can do is climb faster than most people do, because most people never start.

1. **Specifics.** Something concrete and low-stakes. What they had for lunch, what is on their desk.
2. **Preferences with reasons.** Not what they like, but why — the first point at which an answer reveals something.
3. **Memories.** Anything from before they were twenty. Cheap depth: everyone has an answer and nobody has to be clever.
4. **Present difficulty.** What they are finding hard right now. This is the first rung that costs something.
5. **Self-assessment.** How they see themselves, where they think they fall short.
6. **The unresolved.** Grief, regret, fear, the things without a tidy ending.

Each rung is easier if you have just answered the same question. Skipping three rungs is what produces the polite non-answer people mistake for the other person being closed.

## Questions About Who You Were

The cheapest depth available. Start here.

- What was your bedroom like at twelve?
- What did you believe for far too long?
- Who did you want to be like?
- What was the first big thing that went wrong?
- What did your family argue about?
- What were you like at school, honestly?
- What did you do all summer when you were ten?
- Who was the first person outside your family who mattered?
- What is something you were proud of then that seems small now?
- What is something small then that turned out to be enormous?
- What did you think adults knew?
- When did you first feel like you were on your own?
- What did you want to be at fourteen, and what happened to that?
- What is a rule you were raised with that you have kept?
- What is one you dropped?
- What did your house sound like?
- Who was the teacher who mattered, and why?
- What is the first time you remember being properly ashamed?
- What did you get away with?
- What would your twelve-year-old self think of you?

## Questions About How You See Yourself

- What do you think people get wrong about you?
- What is a compliment you do not believe?
- Where are you hardest on yourself?
- What is something you are good at that nobody notices?
- What do you do when nobody is watching that you would not tell people?
- When did you last feel genuinely competent?
- What is a story you tell about yourself that you are not sure is true?
- What do you think you are avoiding finding out?
- What would you want said about you honestly, rather than kindly?
- What is something you want that feels embarrassing to want?
- Who do you compare yourself to?
- What is a version of yourself you have retired?
- What do you think you are getting wrong at the moment?
- What is something you have forgiven yourself for?
- What is something you have not?
- What are you like when you are tired?
- What do you do with anger?
- What is the difference between how you seem and how you are?
- What is a change in yourself you noticed only afterwards?
- What are you proud of that you never get to mention?

## Questions About the People in Your Life

- Who knows you best, and what do they know that others do not?
- Who have you lost touch with that you think about?
- Who do you owe an apology to?
- Who owes you one, and does it still matter?
- What did your parents get right?
- What is something you have never told a friend because it seemed too small?
- Who taught you the most, and did they know?
- What is a friendship that ended without ending?
- Who do you go to first with bad news, and why them?
- What do you find hardest to say to the people closest to you?
- Who have you been unfair to?
- What is something you needed from someone and never asked for?
- Who would you want in the room on the worst day?
- What is the kindest thing anyone has done for you?
- What is something you did for someone that they never knew about?
- Who do you miss that is still alive?
- What do you think you are like to be close to?
- What is a relationship you would repair if it cost you nothing?
- Who changed the direction of your life, and did they mean to?
- What would you say to them now?

## Questions About Fear, Loss and Time

Do not open here. These need the earlier rungs behind them.

- When were you last properly frightened?
- What is a fear you have that you know is disproportionate?
- What are you grieving that nobody would call grief?
- What is the loneliest you have been?
- What do you think happens when we die?
- What would you want your last ordinary day to look like?
- What is something you are putting off that has a deadline you cannot see?
- What have you lost that you have not replaced?
- What are you afraid people will find out?
- What is the closest you have come to giving up on something that mattered?
- What do you think you will regret?
- What did you assume you would have by now?
- What is a version of your life that almost happened?
- Where do you think the time went?
- What is something you would do differently if you had one specific year again?
- What are you not letting yourself feel?
- What is the worst thing that has happened to you that you can talk about?
- What did that change?
- What is something you have survived that you never talk about?
- What would you want people to understand about it?

## Questions About What You Want

- What do you want that you have stopped admitting to?
- What would you do with a year that nobody could ask you about?
- What is enough, for you, specifically?
- What are you working towards that you are no longer sure you want?
- What would have to be true for you to feel settled?
- What is a small thing that would make your life noticeably better?
- What are you waiting for?
- What would you do if you were not worried about looking foolish?
- What is a risk you did not take?
- Would you take it now?
- What do you want to be doing at seventy?
- What would you want to be remembered for by five people rather than by everyone?
- What is something you want to be good at that you are not?
- What are you willing to be bad at in public?
- What would you spend money on without hesitating?
- What is a life you are curious about?
- What would you need to give up to get what you say you want?
- Are you willing to?
- What is the next thing?
- What is the thing after that you are not saying out loud?

## Questions for Someone You Already Trust

These are only for a relationship with history. Asked too early they are intrusive; asked at the right time they are the best questions on this page.

- What do you think I do not see about myself?
- Have I ever hurt you without knowing?
- What do you wish I would ask you about?
- Is there something you have been wanting to say?
- What is different about me from when we met?
- What do you protect me from?
- What is the hardest thing about being close to me?
- When have you been disappointed in me?
- What do you think I am wasting?
- What would you want me to do if you stopped being able to ask?
- What have I got wrong about your life?
- Is there anything you have needed from me that you did not get?
- What do you think I am afraid of?
- What would you tell me if you knew I would not get defensive?
- What is something you have decided not to tell me?
- Do you want to tell me now?
- What is the thing we do not talk about?
- Are we okay?
- What would make this better?
- What do you need?

## Deep Questions That Do Not Work

- **"What is your greatest fear?"** — asks for a summary. Ask when they were last frightened.
- **"What is your biggest regret?"** — same shape. Ask about one specific year.
- **"What is the meaning of life?"** — no foothold. Ask what they have decided is worth their time.
- **"Tell me something nobody knows about you."** — demands the deepest thing first, with no ladder and no reciprocity.
- **"Are you happy?"** — one socially correct answer, and the honest answer sounds like a crisis.
- **"What is your love language?"** — asks for a self-classification. Categories close a conversation; specifics open one.
- **"If you could change one thing about yourself..."** — invites the polished self-criticism people keep ready for interviews.
- **"What are you most passionate about?"** — a networking question wearing a sincere costume.
- **"Do you believe in God?"** — a position, not an experience. "What do you do when you are frightened?" gets closer to the same place.
- **"What is the worst thing you have ever done?"** — asks for a confession without offering one, which is `blocking` in the costume of intimacy.

## What to Do With the Answer

The questions are the easy half. What happens next decides whether you get a second one.

**Ask the follow-up.** Almost nobody does. The first answer is the version they have told before; the second question is where the unrehearsed thing is. "What was the worst part?" or "how long has that been true?" costs nothing.

**Do not fix it.** The urge to solve is about your discomfort, not their problem. Accompanying someone in something unfixable is the whole job, and it feels like doing nothing precisely because the doing is not the point.

**Do not match it.** "That happened to me too", followed by your version, is a topic change that feels like empathy. The tell is that they never get back to what they were saying.

**Receive rather than assess.** `be-thankful` here means treating what arrives as usable rather than evaluating it. Reacting to how heavy something was — rather than to its content — is how people learn what not to tell you.

**Let it be unresolved.** Not every answer needs a landing. Sitting with something open is a real contribution, and the silence after a hard answer is usually more useful than anything you could put in it.

## Practise the Following-Up Part

**Building from their last words.** `last-word-response` forces your reply to start from your partner's final words, which makes changing the subject structurally impossible. It is two minutes long and it is the whole skill above.
