# Questions for Couples: 124 That Update What You Know

Every other list of questions on this site is about building trust with somebody who has none of it yet. This one is the opposite problem, and it needs a different tool.

With a partner, trust is not the constraint. You have plenty. **The constraint is that you stopped updating.**

## The Real Failure Is a Stale Model

Somewhere in the second year, you finish building a model of the other person — what they think about their job, their mother, having children, the way they want a Saturday to go — and then you largely stop checking it. The model is good. It was accurate when you built it. It is why you can order for them and buy the right present and finish their sentences, and finishing their sentences is exactly the problem.

Because they kept changing, and you have been answering on their behalf.

This is `blocking` in its quietest form. Blocking on this site is refusing what somebody offers — and the version that damages long relationships is not refusing an offer but **pre-empting one**: deciding what they were going to say, responding to that, and never finding out. Nobody notices it happening. It presents as knowing each other well.

`active-listening` is the corrective, and in a long relationship it is much harder than it is with a stranger, because with a stranger you have no model to fall back on. You are forced to actually listen. With a partner you can run the whole conversation on cached data and it sounds completely normal.

## The Only Test You Need

Before asking anything on this page, run one check.

**Could you confidently answer this on their behalf?**

If the answer is no, it is a fine question and ask it. If the answer is **yes, that is the question worth asking** — and you should ask it precisely because you think you already know, and then find out whether the answer you were carrying is still the right one.

That is the whole method. The interesting questions in a long relationship are not the exotic ones. They are the ordinary ones you stopped asking around the time you became certain of the answer.

## How Well Do You Know Each Other?

The test above is a thought experiment. This is the version you can actually run, and it is the only game on this page.

**Both of you write down what you think the other will say. Then you both answer. Then compare.** That is it. The scoring is irrelevant and the disagreements are the entire point — every mismatch is a place where your model of them is out of date, which is exactly what this page claims is the problem.

It is worth saying what usually happens, because couples are often braced for the wrong outcome. The mismatches are rarely dramatic. They are almost always small and recent: a preference that shifted, an opinion that softened, something they have started minding that they did not use to. Which is the point — the model does not fail all at once, it drifts.

- What would they say is their favourite meal at the moment?
- What are they most looking forward to in the next month?
- What would they say is the most stressful part of their week?
- What would they choose to do with an entirely free day?
- What is their honest opinion of my closest friend?
- What would they say we argue about most?
- What do they think is my most annoying habit?
- What would they say they need more of?
- What is the last thing that made them properly laugh?
- What would they say is the best decision we have made together?
- What are they secretly hoping I have arranged for their birthday?
- What would they say they are worried about at the moment?
- What is their least favourite thing about our home?
- What would they say has changed most about them in five years?
- What would they save first from a fire, after people?
- What do they think I do not appreciate enough?
- What would they say is their proudest achievement?
- What would they change about their job tomorrow if they could?
- What is the compliment they most want to hear?
- What would they say is the thing I do that makes them feel closest to me?

**The rule that makes it useful rather than a quiz:** when you get one wrong, ask when it changed. That question turns a mismatch into the actual conversation, and it is the reason to do this at all. Getting it wrong is not a failure of attention — it is information arriving late, and late is much better than never.

## Questions for Couples

The general set. Ordinary on purpose — each one is something you probably think you know.

- What has been the best part of the last month for you?
- What are you looking forward to that I might not have noticed?
- What is something you have changed your mind about this year?
- What would you like a larger share of in your week?
- What are you carrying at the moment that you have downplayed to me?
- What would you do with a free weekend if I were away?
- What is something small I do that makes your day easier?
- What is a thing you used to enjoy that has quietly dropped off?
- What would you like us to do more of?
- What do you think I have been worrying about lately?
- What is something you are proud of that nobody congratulated you for?
- What do you need after a difficult day, specifically?
- What is a decision we made that you would make differently now?
- What have you been putting off telling me because it is not urgent?
- What is your honest view of how we spend our weekends?
- What is something you would like to be better at?
- What do you think we are good at that other couples find hard?
- What would make the next year better than this one?
- What is a small thing that has annoyed you more than once?
- What do you wish I asked you about more often?
- What is something you have noticed about me recently?
- What would you want to change about how we handle money?
- What is a plan you have half-made and not mentioned?
- What do you need from me this week that you have not asked for?

## Deep Questions for Couples

Slower, and best when there is time afterwards. Each of these asks for something specific rather than a summary — a summary is where the honest answer goes to hide.

- When did you last feel genuinely close to me?
- What is something you were afraid to tell me early on that you would say now?
- What do you think I am wrong about, that you have not pushed on?
- What part of yourself do you show me that you show nobody else?
- What have I helped you stop being afraid of?
- What do you think you have given up for this relationship?
- When have you felt most alone while we were in the same room?
- What is a version of your life you sometimes think about?
- What do you think I do not understand about your family?
- What would you want me to do if you were struggling and hiding it?
- What is the kindest thing anybody has ever done for you?
- What do you think we avoid talking about?
- When did you last change your mind because of something I said?
- What do you think worries me that I have never named?
- What has been the hardest year of your life, and where was I in it?
- What do you hope is still true about us in ten years?
- What do you think you need that you find difficult to ask for?
- What is a mistake of mine you have forgiven and not mentioned?
- What do you want to be different about how we argue?
- What would you want said about our relationship by somebody watching it?

## Questions to Ask Your Partner About the Two of You

Aimed at the relationship rather than at either person in it. These are the ones couples skip, because the relationship feels like a fixed object rather than something either of you is choosing daily.

- What has got easier between us that used to be difficult?
- What did you assume about being with me that turned out to be wrong?
- What do you think our friends think of us?
- What is a habit we have got into that neither of us chose?
- What does a good week look like for us specifically?
- What do we do that other people would find strange?
- What is something we tried that did not work and we never discussed?
- What do you think I want from you that I have never said?
- What is the thing we disagree about that will not resolve?
- What would we be like if we had met five years earlier?
- When are we at our best, and can we do that more?
- What is something we used to do that we should start again?
- What do you think has been the turning point for us?
- What would you tell somebody about to start a relationship like ours?
- What is the story about us you tell other people?
- What do you think we take for granted?

## Questions for a Long Relationship

Specifically for couples several years in, where the ordinary questions have been answered so long ago that nobody thinks to check.

- What do you want the next ten years to contain that the last ten did not?
- What is something about your work you have stopped telling me because it is boring to explain?
- What have you got better at that I have not acknowledged?
- What do you miss about who you were before we met?
- What is something you want to do before you are ten years older?
- What do you think is the biggest thing that has changed about you?
- What is something you believe now that would surprise the you who met me?
- What do you want more of that you feel slightly silly asking for?
- What is a friendship of yours you would like to rescue?
- What would you like to be doing on an ordinary Tuesday in five years?
- What do you think we should have sorted out by now?
- What is something you have grown out of?
- What have you stopped hoping for, and should you have?
- What do you think I have got better at?
- What would you want us to protect if everything else got busier?
- What is something you have wanted to say for a while?

## Questions to Ask Your Spouse

Marriage adds a set of questions that dating does not, and most of them are logistical in a way that sounds unromantic and is not. A great deal of what couples experience as drifting apart is actually an unexamined arrangement that suited two people who no longer exist.

- What do you think our division of everything actually is, honestly?
- What is a household job you have quietly resented for years?
- What would you change about how we spend money, if I did not react?
- What do you want retirement to look like, in ordinary detail?
- What do you think we should be saving for that we are not?
- What is your view of how we handle each other's families?
- What do you want to be different about how we host people?
- What would you want to happen if one of us had to stop working?
- What is a tradition of ours you would happily drop?
- What do you want more say in?
- What have you agreed to that you never really wanted?
- What do you think I do more of than you notice, and vice versa?
- What is something we decided once and never revisited?
- What do you want the next decade to be for, specifically?
- What would you want me to know if you were ill?
- What do you think we owe each other that is not obvious?
- What is a conversation we keep starting and never finishing?
- What do you want our home to feel like?
- What is something you would like us to be braver about?
- What do you think has kept this working?

## Questions When Something Is Wrong

For when the distance is already there. Ask one, not eight, and be prepared for the answer to be about you.

- Where have I been letting you down without realising?
- When did this start feeling difficult for you?
- What do you need that you are not getting?
- What do you think I am not hearing?
- What would you want me to stop doing?
- What is the thing you have almost said several times?
- What would make this week easier?
- What do you need me to understand before we try to fix anything?

## The Ones That Cause Fights

Worth naming, because each looks reasonable and lands badly.

**Anything beginning "why do you always".** It is an accusation wearing a question mark, and it gets defended rather than answered.

**"Are you happy?"** Too large, and it demands a verdict on the entire relationship in one word. Ask what has been good and what has been hard this month instead — the same information arrives in a form somebody can actually answer.

**Comparisons to other people's relationships.** Nobody has ever answered these usefully, and the real question underneath — what do you want that we do not have — is available directly.

**Questions asked to make a point.** If you already know the answer and are asking so they will hear themselves say it, that is not a question, and they will know within about two words.

## How to Ask Them

**Not across a table, facing each other.** Side by side is easier — a walk, a drive, washing up. Direct eye contact raises the stakes of every answer, which is useful in a first conversation and counterproductive in a long relationship where somebody needs room to be uncertain out loud.

**Answer first, and answer honestly.** `vulnerability` is being seen without armour, and it cannot be requested one-way. Ask your partner something you have not answered yourself and it lands as an audit.

**Do not fix anything for an hour.** The reflex to solve is the single most common way these conversations end early. What is being offered is information, not a task.

**One question is a good evening.** Twenty is an interview. Any one of these, properly followed, is worth more than the whole page skimmed.

## The Part You Can Practise

The skill underneath all of this is unglamorous: taking in what was actually said rather than what you expected.

`last-word-response` trains it directly — each reply has to be built from the last thing your partner said, which makes it impossible to run the conversation on the model you already had. It is an improv exercise and it feels artificial for about two minutes, at which point people generally notice how much of their listening had been prediction.

For the version of this with somebody you do not know yet, where trust rather than staleness is the constraint, that is [deep questions to ask](/deep-questions-to-ask), and [questions to get to know someone](/questions-to-get-to-know-someone) for the earliest stage of all.
