# Conversation Starters: 100 That Survive the Second Exchange

Most people looking for conversation starters have diagnosed the wrong problem. A conversation died, the opening was the last thing they remember choosing, so the opening gets the blame.

Openings almost never kill conversations. **A starter buys you exactly one exchange** — they answer, and then you are both standing in the same silence you were in before, except now it is worse because you have used your material. What happens at exchange two is the entire game, and no list of openers will help with it.

So: 100 starters below, sorted by the room you are in, because you did come here for those. And then the part that actually decides whether the conversation goes anywhere.

## What Makes a Starter Work

Three things, and none of them is being interesting.

**It has to be answerable without effort.** A starter is an `offers` — something you hand over for the other person to build with. Judge it by what they can do with it, not by how it sounds coming out of your mouth. "What's the most interesting thing that's happened to you this year?" is a fine question and a terrible opener, because answering it well is work and answering it badly is embarrassing.

**Specific beats general, every time.** "How's it going?" produces "yeah, good." "How was the drive over?" produces an actual sentence, because there is something to describe.

**The obvious one is the good one.** `obvious-choice` says your first thought is connected to the situation and your second is connected to your ego. The opener you reach for when you stop trying to be memorable is almost always better, because the other person can tell the difference between a question and an audition.

## Starters for a Room Where You Know Nobody

- How do you know everyone here?
- What brought you along tonight?
- How was getting here?
- Have you been to one of these before?
- What have you got on this week?
- Whereabouts are you based?
- What is the best thing about that bit of town?
- What did you do today before this?
- Are you staying for the whole thing?
- What made you come out rather than stay in?
- Do you know what happens next in this thing?
- Has anyone told you where the drinks are?
- What is your read on this room so far?
- What did you nearly do instead of coming?
- Have you eaten, or are we both pretending?
- Is this your normal sort of evening?
- What is the last thing like this you went to?
- Who should I be talking to here?
- What do you do when you get home from something like this?
- What is the plan after?

## Funny Conversation Starters

The zany ones are the ones that die. "If you were a kitchen appliance, which would you be?" gets a laugh, an answer, and then nothing — the joke is over and there is nowhere to stand.

The funny ones that actually run are specific and mundane. Comedy comes from the detail, and detail is something the other person can add to.

- What is in your bag right now that needs explaining?
- What is a chore you secretly enjoy?
- What is the most useless skill you have?
- What is something you are irrationally loyal to?
- What is the worst haircut you have had?
- What is a film everyone loves that you cannot stand?
- What is the pettiest reason you have fallen out with someone?
- What is the strangest job you have heard of someone doing?
- What did you get told off for most as a kid?
- What is a word you always mispronounce?
- What is something you are bad at and have made peace with?
- What is the worst present you have ever received?
- What is a rule in your house that would confuse a visitor?
- What is the last thing you did that your fourteen-year-old self would find pathetic?
- What is something everyone else seems to enjoy that you find baffling?
- What is the most embarrassing thing in your search history that you can say out loud?
- What is a lie you tell regularly and harmlessly?
- What would you be famous for if you had to be famous for something stupid?
- What is the last thing you argued about that did not matter?
- What is something you own that you would be embarrassed to explain?

## Starters That Work at Work

- What are you working on that is actually interesting?
- What is taking up the most time this week?
- What is the part of your job people misunderstand?
- How did you end up on this team?
- What did you do before here?
- What is something you have got much better at recently?
- What is the last thing that went unexpectedly well?
- Who else should I be talking to about this?
- What is the thing you would fix if it were up to you?
- What is coming up that you are dreading?
- What has changed here since you started?
- What is the best meeting you have been in this month?
- What do you do that nobody else on the team does?
- Where are you working from at the moment?
- What is your first hour of the day like?
- What is something you have learned here that you did not expect to?

## Starters for a Group or a Table

Everyone can answer these in a sentence, which is what stops one person taking the floor for four minutes.

- What is everyone's worst job?
- What is the best thing anyone has eaten this week?
- What did everyone assume they would be doing by now?
- What is a small thing that improves any day?
- Where would everyone go with a free flight tomorrow?
- What is something you have never got round to learning?
- What is something everyone here has done that nobody admits to?
- What is the last thing anyone learned that stuck?
- What is a hill somebody would die on?
- What is everyone's most-used app that is not messaging?
- What is the best advice anyone here has been given?
- What is a thing everyone nearly did instead of coming out?
- What is the strangest place anyone here has lived?
- What is something you have all changed your mind about?
- What is the best thing about where everyone lives?
- What is one thing you would each bring back from ten years ago?

## Starters for People You Already Know

The problem with old friends is not awkwardness, it is that the conversation defaults to a status update. These skip it.

- What is the thing you have not told anyone about yet?
- What are you avoiding at the moment?
- What have you changed your mind about since I last saw you?
- What is taking up most of your head?
- What is everyone putting off this week?
- What is going well that you have not mentioned because it is small?
- What would you do with a free month?
- What did everyone give up that they still think about?
- Who have you been thinking about that you have not contacted?
- What is the last thing that properly surprised you?
- What is the best thing that has happened since we spoke?
- What is something you are looking forward to that is months away?
- What have you been getting wrong lately?
- What do you want more of at the moment?
- What is a thing you keep meaning to start?
- What should I ask you about that I never do?

## Starters for Kids and Family

Shorter, concrete, and with an obvious way in. The dinner-table versions work because nobody has to perform.

- What was the funniest thing that happened today?
- What was the most boring bit of your day?
- Who did you sit with at lunch?
- What is something you found out today?
- What would you do if you were in charge of the house for a day?
- What is the best thing in your bag right now?
- What is a rule you think is unfair?
- What would you like us to do at the weekend?
- What is something you are looking forward to?
- What made someone laugh today?
- If you could swap jobs with anyone here, who?
- What was the best thing you ate today?

## Why Conversation Starters Fail

Almost never at the opening. Here is where it actually goes wrong.

**The answer gets received and then dropped.** They say something, you say "oh, nice", and you both start looking for the next question. Two more rounds of that and the conversation is a quiz.

**You answer with your own version instead of building on theirs.** They mention a holiday, you mention yours. It feels like participating. `steering` is the improv name for it — accepting on the surface while redirecting to your own material — and the tell is that they never get back to what they were saying.

**You reach for the next starter.** This is the one caused by lists like this one. Having questions in reserve makes it tempting to fire the next one rather than sit in what was just said, and a sequence of unrelated good questions is an interview.

**Nothing is offered back.** A question with no disclosure attached is a request. Two or three in a row and the other person can feel the imbalance even if they could not name it.

## What to Do After They Answer

This is the part that would have fixed the conversation you are trying to fix.

**Build from their last words.** Whatever they just said, your next line should come out of it. Not a new topic, not your equivalent story — an extension of theirs. `last-word-response` is an exercise built entirely around this, and it works because it makes the alternative structurally impossible.

**Ask about the detail, not the headline.** They say "we finally moved out of my mum's place in March." The headline is moving. The detail is the mum, or the March, or the "finally". Most people ask about the move.

**Say something back.** One sentence about yourself, at roughly the depth they just offered. Not a story, not a matching anecdote — evidence that this is an exchange rather than a survey.

**Let a pause run one beat longer than feels comfortable.** People usually fill it with the second half of what they were saying, which is the half worth having. `be-thankful` here means treating what arrives as usable rather than assessing it — including the silence.

## Practise the Second Exchange

**Building from what they said.** `last-word-response` forces every reply to start from your partner's final words. Two minutes of it and you will notice how often your instinct is to change the subject.
