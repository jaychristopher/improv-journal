# Questions to Ask a Girl: Why the List Is Not the Problem

Almost every list under this heading is sold as a script — the right sequence of inputs for a specific kind of person, as though the answer depended on who is opposite you.

It does not. **Nothing about getting to know somebody changes according to their gender.** The questions that work with a woman you have just met are the questions that work with anybody you have just met, for the same reasons, and a list that implies otherwise has already misdescribed the problem.

Which is worth saying plainly, because the framing is not harmless. A script turns the other person into a lock to be opened, and people can tell within about two questions when that is what is happening.

## What Is Actually Going Wrong

The real problem behind this search is almost never a shortage of questions. It is one of two things.

**You are asking and not answering.** A question you would not answer yourself, or have not answered, converts an exchange into an interview. People answer interviews accurately and reveal nothing, because there is no reason to — nothing has been offered in return, so nothing is owed. `trust` on this site is an accumulated estimate built from small exchanges that go unpunished, and an exchange requires two people putting something in.

**You are asking about her rather than about now.** "What are you passionate about" requires a self-assessment before it can be answered, and self-assessments come out rehearsed. "What did you nearly do instead of coming tonight" has an answer already sitting there.

Fix those two and the specific questions barely matter. Fail to fix them and no list rescues it.

That is also why this page is shorter than its competitors, which run to three hundred questions and up. The volume is not the useful part, and the site has plenty of it elsewhere if you want it — what is below is the set that survives the two failures above, plus the follow-ups that do more work than any opening question.

## The Only Rule

**Answer everything you ask, at the same depth, without being asked to.**

That is the whole technique. It is not a nicety — it is the mechanism. Every answer you give at a slightly greater depth than the last gives the other person permission to match it, and the conversation escalates without anybody deciding to escalate. Ask ten questions and answer none and you have conducted a survey.

`offers` on this site is the idea that everything you say is something the other person can build with. Judge your questions by what she can do with them, not by how they sound when you say them. And `obvious-choice` is worth remembering when you are tempted to be impressive: your first, plainest question is connected to the situation, and your second is connected to how you want to appear. The plain one works better, every time.

## Questions for the First Conversation

Low cost, specific, and answerable without effort. Nobody has to be interesting.

- What has your week been like, honestly?
- What is the best thing about where you live now?
- What were you like at school?
- What do you do when you have an afternoon to yourself?
- What have you been watching or reading lately?
- What did you want to do when you were younger?
- What do you usually order?
- What is coming up that you are pleased about?
- What is something small that has made your week better?
- What are you like in the mornings?

## Questions That Get Past Small Talk

The move here is not a bigger question. It is picking up something she mentioned in passing and asking about that instead of the topic she offered.

- What made you choose it over the alternative?
- What do people usually get wrong about that?
- What is something you have changed your mind about lately?
- What are you working on that has nothing to do with work?
- What would you do with a year and no obligations?
- What is a rule you have for yourself that nobody taught you?
- What do you do that would surprise somebody who has just met you?
- What is the best decision you have made in the last few years?

## Questions That Show You Were Listening

Not a list to work through. These are the follow-ups that turn any answer into a conversation, and using one is worth more than the next ten questions from any list.

- What was that like?
- How did you end up there?
- What did you make of it at the time?
- What would you do differently now?
- Was that what you expected?
- What is the bit you leave out when you tell that story?
- Who else was there?
- How long had that been building?
- What did you decide after that?
- Do you still think about it?

## Funny and Light Questions

Nothing here asks anybody to perform, which is the mistake most funny lists make. The comedy is in ordinary life reported accurately.

- What is the most useless thing you own?
- What is a strong opinion you hold about something trivial?
- What is the worst haircut you have lived through?
- What is something everyone likes that you find baffling?
- What is your most irrational fear?
- What is the worst film you have watched to the end?
- What is a word you cannot say properly?
- What is the least cool thing you genuinely enjoy?
- What is in your bag that needs explaining?
- What is a chore you secretly like?
- What would your search history suggest you are up to?
- What is something you are weirdly competitive about?
- What is your worst habit that you have no intention of fixing?
- What is the most you have overreacted to something small?

## Deeper Questions, Once There Is Trust

Not for a first hour. These work when several exchanges have already gone unpunished, and only if you are answering them too.

- What are you proud of that nobody congratulates you for?
- When did you last change your mind about something that mattered?
- What is something you have never been good at accepting?
- What has been the best year of your life so far?
- What would you tell yourself at eighteen?

## Questions for Somebody You Like

Warmer, and none of them force a declaration before either person is ready.

- What is the nicest thing anybody has done for you?
- What are you hopeless at?
- What would surprise me about you?
- What is the best conversation you have had recently?
- What are you looking for, if you know?
- What would a good version of the next few months look like?

## Questions That Do Not Work

**Anything that tests her.** Trick questions, hypotheticals designed to reveal a "type", anything with a right answer. Being assessed is unpleasant and obvious.

**Anything you have not answered yourself.** The single most common failure. If you would find it intrusive, it is intrusive.

**Compliments disguised as questions.** "Why are you so..." puts somebody in the position of either accepting or denying a claim about themselves, and both are awkward.

**Rapid-fire anything.** Ten questions in five minutes is an interrogation regardless of how good the questions are. One question and four minutes of following it up is a conversation.

**Anything about exes, body, or money, early.** These are not mysterious. They cost something to answer and nothing has been built yet that would make paying it reasonable.

## How to Ask Them

**Answer first sometimes.** Not always — that becomes a monologue — but often enough that it is visibly an exchange.

**Follow the interesting thing, not your list.** If an answer contains something better than what you planned to ask next, abandon the plan. The list has done its job.

**Let a silence sit.** Filling every pause reads as nerves and stops her adding the second half of an answer, which is usually the good half. `be-present` is mostly this: staying in the moment that exists rather than fetching the next one.

**Notice when to stop.** Short answers, no questions back, no elaboration — that is information, and it is not a cue to try a better question.

## The Part You Can Practise

Everything above rests on one skill, and it is not asking. It is what you do with the answer while it is still arriving.

`active-listening` is attending to what was actually said rather than composing your reply underneath it. `last-word-response` trains it directly by requiring each reply to be built from the other person's last words, which makes drafting impossible. Most people trying it discover how much of their listening had been preparation.
