# Most Likely To Questions: 182 That Land as a Compliment

Every other question game asks about the person answering. This one is different in a way that gets overlooked: **the answer is about somebody else in the room**, and they do not get a say in it.

That single structural fact explains everything about how the game goes well and how it goes wrong.

## What It Is Actually For

The pleasure of being named is not being funny. It is **being recognised**.

"Most likely to reply at 2am" means somebody has noticed a real thing about you. That is a small gift, and it is the entire reason a group enjoys this — not the joke, but the evidence that people have been paying attention. `be-thankful` on this site is about treating what somebody brings as worth having, and being named well is the purest version of that: you are told, in public, that you are legible to the people around you.

Which sets the test for a good one. **Does being named feel like being seen, or like being caught?** Those are near neighbours and the game lives entirely on one side of the line.

## Why It Turns Into a Roast

The failure has a mechanism worth naming.

`status` on this site is the constant, mostly invisible negotiation of who is up and who is down in any interaction. Most likely to is a status game whether anybody intends it or not, because each round assigns a label to a specific person by group vote. When the labels are affectionate, status stays level and everyone gets a turn at being noticed. When the labels name a deficiency — most likely to get fired, most likely to be single forever — the group has now voted on somebody's standing, and there is no comfortable reply.

The named person cannot object without seeming unable to take a joke, which means the cost is invisible to everyone else. That is what makes this the highest-risk question format there is, and why the damage usually shows up later rather than in the room.

**Three rules keep it on the right side.**

- **Name habits, not shortcomings.** Always late is a habit. Unreliable is a character judgement. They describe the same behaviour and land completely differently.
- **Everybody gets named.** If the same two people are chosen for everything, stop — the game has become about them, and the quiet ones are now spectators at their own party.
- **Give right of reply, briefly.** Whoever is named gets one sentence. It converts the label from a verdict into a conversation, and it is usually the funniest part.

## The Ones That Damage a Group

Worth naming, because each is common and each costs more than it returns.

**Anything naming a failure.** Most likely to get sacked, to be single forever, to relapse, to fail. The group has now voted on somebody's future in front of them.

**Anything about appearance or money.** No good version exists. There is no reply that does not cost the person something.

**Anything one person has actually done.** If the room is thinking of a specific incident, this is not a game, it is a reminder with a format.

**Playing it with people who have just met.** Nobody can be recognised for something they have had no time to demonstrate, so the answers become guesses based on appearance — which is the worst possible first impression to hand somebody.

## How to Run It

**Everybody points at once, on three.** Simultaneous voting is faster and stops later votes following earlier ones, which is what turns a round into a pile-on.

**Named person gets a sentence.** Not a defence — a reply. This is where most of the laughs actually come from.

**Rotate who asks.** Whoever holds the list steers the game, and a list read by one person all evening reflects one person's sense of humour.

**Stop before the good ones run out.** Fifteen strong pairs beats forty where the last twenty were reaching.

## The Part You Can Practise

The whole skill here is making somebody else look good in public, quickly, without it sounding like flattery.

`gift-giving` is the improv exercise built precisely for that: you hand your scene partner something that makes them more interesting than you. It is the reflex this game rewards and the one that makes people want you at the next thing — `ensemble` awareness is mostly this, done repeatedly and without announcement.

For the version where the answers are about the person answering rather than about the room, [would you rather questions](/would-you-rather-questions) and [this or that questions](/this-or-that-questions) are lower risk and warm a group faster — and [party games](/party-games) has the whole set, sorted by group size.
