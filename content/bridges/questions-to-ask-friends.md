# Questions to Ask Friends: 120 That Go Somewhere

Most lists of questions to ask friends are optimised for the answer — questions that produce an interesting sentence. That is the wrong thing to optimise for, because the answer is not the point. What happens in the ninety seconds *after* the answer is the point, and a lot of very interesting questions produce nothing at all afterwards.

Below are 120 questions grouped by what they open rather than by how deep they sound, plus the part the lists leave out: which questions are actively bad, and why.

## Why Most Question Lists Fail

A question is an `offers` — a thing you hand your friend to build with. That reframes what makes one good.

**A good question gives them somewhere to stand.** "What's your biggest regret?" is enormous, unanchored, and most people answer it with a summary. "What's something you'd do differently if you had that year again?" is the same question with a floor under it.

**A specific question is easier to answer than a general one.** People experience "tell me about yourself" as work and "what's in your fridge right now" as easy, and the second one reliably produces more.

**A question you would not answer yourself is a bad question.** This is the whole thing, and the section below is about it.

## The One Thing That Decides Whether a Question Lands

You have to go first.

`vulnerability` on this site is being seen without armour, and it is not something you can ask someone else to do on your behalf. A question that asks for more openness than you have offered reads as an interview, and people answer interviews carefully.

The move is small: answer your own question first, or answer it immediately after they do, at the same depth. `trust` is an accumulated estimate built from many small exchanges of exactly this kind — one person goes slightly further than is comfortable, nothing bad happens, and the estimate updates. Asking without offering never updates anything.

## Warm-Up Questions

Low stakes, specific, and answerable by anyone. Use these to start, not to finish.

- What's in your fridge that shouldn't be?
- What did you last look up on your phone?
- What's the best thing you've eaten this month?
- What's your most-used app that isn't messaging?
- What did you do last Sunday, hour by hour?
- What's something small you've bought recently that you love?
- What's your commute like?
- What are you watching at the moment, and is it any good?
- What's the last thing that made you laugh out loud?
- What's on your desk right now?
- What's a smell you like?
- What's your default order somewhere?
- What did you nearly wear today instead?
- What's the last photo you took?
- What time did you actually go to bed?
- What's a small thing that's been annoying you?
- What's the last thing you fixed?
- What are you looking forward to this week?
- What's playing in your car?
- What's the last thing you finished?

## Questions That Get Past the Update

"How's work" produces a status report. These produce a conversation.

- What's the part of your job you'd keep if you could only keep one?
- Who at work do you actually like?
- What's a thing you're good at that nobody notices?
- What are you avoiding at the moment?
- What's been taking up the most space in your head lately?
- What's a decision you're sitting on?
- What would you do with an unexpected free week?
- What's changed for you in the last year that you didn't expect?
- Is there anything you keep meaning to start?
- What's something you've stopped doing that you miss?
- What are you better at now than you were two years ago?
- What's the last thing you changed your mind about?
- What's an ordinary part of your week that you genuinely enjoy?
- Who have you been thinking about lately that you haven't contacted?
- What's a problem you've solved recently that you're pleased with?
- What do you do when you can't sleep?
- What's something you're doing purely because you always have?
- What would you want more of, if it were free?
- What are you worried you're getting wrong?
- What's the most recent thing that surprised you about yourself?

## Questions About the Past

Memory questions are the cheapest depth there is. Nobody has to be clever, and everyone has an answer.

- Who was your first proper friend?
- What was your family's rule that other families didn't have?
- What did you want to be at fourteen?
- What was your first job's uniform?
- Where did you go on holiday as a child?
- What was the first thing you bought with your own money?
- What subject were you unexpectedly good at?
- Who was the teacher who mattered?
- What did your parents argue about?
- What was the first film that frightened you?
- What did you do all summer when you were ten?
- What's a place you've driven past a hundred times and never stopped?
- What was the last thing you owned that fell apart?
- Which of your childhood friends would you most like an update on?
- What did you believe for far too long?
- What was the first big thing that went wrong?
- What did your house smell like?
- What was the first thing you were properly obsessed with?
- Who did you want to be like?

Do not open with these. They work once the room is warm and you have already answered something at this level yourself.

- Where do you think you're hardest on yourself?
- What do you think people get wrong about you?
- What's a compliment you don't believe?
- What's something you've forgiven yourself for?
- What's something you haven't?
- Who do you compare yourself to?
- What do you think you're avoiding finding out?
- What would you want said about you, honestly, not kindly?
- When did you last feel genuinely competent?

- What's something you want that feels embarrassing to want?

## Questions for a Long Drive or a Late Night

The conditions matter more than the questions. Side by side, no eye contact, and nowhere else to be — people say things in a car at night that they would not say across a table.

- What do you think happens when we die?
- What's the best decision you've ever made by accident?
- If you had to move abroad next month, where?
- What's something you believe that most people you know don't?
- What would you do with your time if money were solved?
- What's a life you almost had?
- What do you think you'd have been like if you'd been born somewhere else?
- Who's the most interesting person you've ever met?
- What's the strangest thing that's ever happened to you?
- What would you tell yourself at twenty, knowing they wouldn't listen?
- What do you think you're wrong about right now?
- Is there anything you'd want to be famous for?
- What's the closest you've come to a completely different life?
- What's a small thing that would make your life noticeably better?
- What's something you'd never do again?
- What do you think is the point?
- What's something you've never said out loud because it sounds stupid?
- If you could know one true thing about your future, would you want to?
- What do you think you'll be like at seventy?
- What's the best conversation you've ever had?

## Questions for a Friend You've Drifted From

The problem here is not the question. It is that both people know the gap exists and neither wants to name it, so the conversation stays on safe ground and confirms the drift.

- What have I missed?
- What's different about your life now?
- Is there something going on that I should know about?
- What have you been up to that you're actually pleased with?
- Do you still see the people we used to see?
- What made you think of me?
- Has anything happened that I'd be sad to have missed?
- Are we okay?
- What would make this easier to keep up?
- Is there a version of this where we talk more?
- What did you think when I got in touch?
- What's the thing you'd tell me if we still spoke every week?
- Did something happen, or did we just stop?
- Can we do this again in a month?
- What's the last thing you remember us doing?
- Who do you talk to now?
- What do you think I've got wrong about your life?
- Is there anything you needed from me that you didn't get?
- What would you want me to know before we pick this up again?
- What's changed about you that I'd notice?

## Questions That Are Actually Bad

Every list includes some of these. They are worth knowing about because each one fails in an instructive way.

- **"What's your biggest fear?"** — too large to answer honestly, so it gets answered performatively. Spiders, or something that sounds like therapy.
- **"Tell me something nobody knows about you."** — demands the deepest thing first and puts the other person on the spot to be interesting.
- **"What's your love language?"** — asks for a self-classification rather than an experience. Categories close conversations; stories open them.
- **"If you could have dinner with anyone, dead or alive..."** — produces a name and then nothing. Every possible answer is a dead end.
- **"What's your biggest regret?"** — see above. Ask about a specific year instead.
- **"Are you happy?"** — has one socially correct answer and makes the honest answer feel like a crisis.
- **"What are your goals for the next five years?"** — an interview question. People answer it with their CV.
- **"What's the worst thing you've ever done?"** — asks for a confession without offering one, which is `blocking` wearing the costume of intimacy.
- **"Why are you single?"** — asks someone to account for their life to you.
- **"Don't you think you should..."** — advice with a question mark on it. See below.

## How to Ask

The questions are the easy part. What you do after the answer decides whether you get another one.

**Receive before you respond.** Take in what was actually said, including the part they nearly did not say. `be-thankful` on this site means treating what arrives as usable rather than evaluating it — a friend telling you something awkward or unflattering is handing you information about their real life.

**Do not trade.** "That happened to me too" and then your version. It feels like empathy and functions as a topic change. The tell is that they never get back to what they were saying.

**Ask the second question.** Almost nobody does. The first answer is the summary; the second question is where the actual thing is. "What was the worst part?" or "How long has that been going on?" costs nothing and changes the conversation entirely.

**Let it stay unresolved.** Sitting with something unfixed is a real contribution and it feels like doing nothing, which is why people reach for advice instead. If you are not sure which conversation you are in, ask: "do you want thoughts, or do you want to be annoyed about it for a bit?"

## Practise the Receiving Part

**Receiving without evaluating.** `gift-giving` hands over an undefined object that the receiver names and values specifically. It is two minutes long and it is the entire skill above.

**Extending rather than replacing.** `last-word-response` forces your reply to build from your partner's final words, which makes changing the subject structurally impossible.
