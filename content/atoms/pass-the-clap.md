**Trains:** shared timing. Two people clap at the same instant, round a circle, until the group stops passing a clap and starts keeping one.

## Setup

Stand in a circle. Turn to the person beside you and clap **together** — one sound, not two. They turn to the next person and do the same. The clap travels round.

Once it is moving, allow it to reverse: a player may turn back the way it came instead of continuing.

## Why it works

Nothing in the game can be done alone. A clap that arrives early or late is two sounds, and everyone hears it. There is no way to fake having been present.

That makes it the cheapest available lesson in what `signal` means — you are not producing a clap, you are matching one, and matching requires reading the tiny preparatory movement that comes before your partner's hands meet. Players who watch hands are late. Players who watch the whole person are on time.

It is `mirroring` compressed into a single instant and shared by twelve people instead of two.

## Side-coaching

"One sound." "Look at their eyes, not their hands." "Breathe together first." "Don't rush it round — the speed comes on its own." "If it was two claps, do that one again."

## What to notice

The circle almost always speeds up without deciding to. That acceleration is the group finding a shared tempo, and it is the same phenomenon as an ensemble finding a rhythm in a show. Then, at some speed, it breaks — and where it breaks tells you how much attention the room actually has today. Coaching it back down to the speed where every clap is one sound is more useful than getting it fast.

## Common failures

**Clapping at your partner rather than with them.** Produces a rally of solo claps travelling round. The tell is two distinct sounds. Slow down until they merge.

**Anticipating the reversal.** Once reversals are allowed, players start guessing. Guessing right looks impressive and is the opposite of the exercise — the point is to be available for either.

**Silence around a miss.** A group that goes quiet when the clap breaks is telling you it is not safe to be the one who broke it. Restart it immediately and cheerfully; the recovery is more instructive than the run.

## Variations

- **Add a second clap** travelling the other way. The moment two claps meet is the game's best problem and the group has to solve it without discussion.
- **Silent version.** Hands meet without sound. Removes the audible feedback and forces the reading of preparation, which is the actual skill.
- **Eyes-only pass.** No turning, no gesture — send the clap across the circle by looking. Much harder, and a direct rehearsal for the specificity `initiation` needs.
