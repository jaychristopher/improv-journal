**Will Hines. *How to Be the Greatest Improviser on Earth.* Self-published, 2016.**

A practitioner's guide organizing improv around behavioral mantras: Be Present, Be Changeable, Be Brave, Be Playful. Deeply influenced by Del Close and Keith Johnstone, translated for contemporary UCB-lineage performers.

## What is actually in it

Short chapters, one behavioural instruction each, written in the voice of somebody talking after class rather than lecturing. The title is a joke and the book is not — it is among the most practically useful things written about improv in the last decade, and it is self-published, which is why it appears on no recommended-reading list assembled by an institution.

Its distinguishing move is refusing to describe scenes. Almost every improv book explains what a good scene contains. Hines writes about what a performer is doing internally in the seconds before they speak, which is the part that actually goes wrong and the part nobody can see from the audience.

## Who it rewards

Somebody who already takes classes and keeps hitting the same wall. It assumes the mechanics and addresses the habits, so a complete beginner will nod along without changing anything, and an experienced performer will recognise themselves uncomfortably often.

**Worth knowing before you read it here.** The naming convention this site uses for its principles overlaps directly with his chapter titles — Be Present, Be Changeable, Be Brave are his headings as well as ours. That is influence rather than coincidence, and it means agreement between the two is not independent corroboration.

**Key concepts drawn from here:**
- "Be Present" as a chapter-level principle — matches our second principle exactly
- "Be Changeable" — "It's not just changing the present. It's changing the past. You are adjusting who you are with every line."
- "Be Brave" — courage to initiate, to act before ready
- The fact/opinion distinction in accepting offers — accept facts, retain agency over opinions
- Initiation etiquette — "When you initiate, tell the other person who they are"
- The cult of the obvious (extending Johnstone)
