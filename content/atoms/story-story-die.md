A line of players tells a single story, and a conductor decides who is telling it. The finger moves without warning, mid-sentence and often mid-word, and whoever it lands on carries on from exactly there. Fumble, hesitate or break the grammar and you are out. The survivor finishes the story.

## What it trains

Attention with no gaps in it. Every other narrative exercise gives you a turn you can see coming; this one removes that entirely, so the only way to survive is to be composing the sentence continuously whether or not you are speaking. That is much closer to what a scene actually demands than a circle where you know your turn is four people away.

It also makes `hesitation` audible to a whole room. A pause here is not a subtle loss of energy, it is elimination, which is the clearest possible feedback that the pause was the problem rather than the content.

## Running it well

- **Point mid-word, not between sentences.** Handing over at a full stop is a different and much easier game. The mid-word handover is where the listening happens.
- **Judge grammar, not quality.** The rule is that the sentence must continue coherently. Eliminating somebody for a boring choice teaches the room to search for interesting ones, which produces exactly the hesitation the game punishes.
- **Take a suggestion for the title only.** More than that and the players are trying to hit a target instead of listening.

## The honest objection

Elimination punishes the thing the game claims to train.

A player who has been knocked out learns to be careful, and careful is the opposite of the fluency being asked for. Watch a second round and the surviving players get noticeably safer — shorter clauses, blander choices, nothing that could trip them — because the incentive is not to tell a good story but to avoid being the one who breaks it. `performing-cleverness` is not the failure mode here; timidity is.

There is a version that fixes it: keep the pointing, drop the elimination, and run it for four minutes with everybody in for the whole time. It is less exciting to watch and considerably better practice. Use the competitive version as a show game and the other one in a workshop.
