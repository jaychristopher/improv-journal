**Charna Halpern. *Art by Committee: A Guide to Advanced Improvisation.* Meriwether Publishing, 2006.**

Twelve years after Truth in Comedy, and written alone. That matters more than a sequel usually does, because the earlier book was co-authored with Del Close and reads in places like a manifesto for a form still being argued over. This one is written by the person who kept the theatre running for the two decades afterwards, and it is correspondingly less interested in defending the Harold than in reporting what happened to it.

The chapter that earns the book its place here is "The New Harold". The form as first published has three beats, three group games and a rigid shape, and almost nobody performs it that way any more. Halpern says so plainly and describes what companies actually do instead — which openings survived, which beat structures loosened, and where the original rigidity was load-bearing rather than incidental.

The rest is advanced material in the ordinary sense: how to build an opening that generates rather than decorates, how connections between beats get made without being signposted, and what group mind looks like when it is working and when it is being performed. The accompanying DVD footage of UCB performing the Harold, Fey and Poehler among them, is the reason a lot of people bought it.

## Who it rewards

Performers already running long form, and especially anybody performing the Harold who suspects the version they were taught is not the version anybody plays. That gap is the book's most useful contribution and it is not documented anywhere else with this authority.

![Two marks for the same form at different positions, with the distance between them bracketed in accent.](/images/two-versions-of-one-form.svg)

*Nothing here says which version is better; the book's contribution is that the distance exists at all.*


Beginners will find almost nothing. It is advanced material by design, with little scaffolding and no route in, and the vocabulary assumes the earlier book.

**The honest caveat.** It carries the authority of the iO lineage while being one half of the partnership that produced it. Close was dead seven years by publication, and where *Truth in Comedy* reads as two people arguing a form into existence, this reads as the surviving founder describing what the form became. That is valuable and it is a settlement rather than a debate — the disagreements that made the earlier book alive have been resolved offstage, and the reader is told the outcome without seeing the argument.

**Key concepts drawn from here:**
- The New Harold — how the published form differs from what companies now run, and which constraints turned out to matter
- Openings judged by what they yield later in the set rather than by how they look
- Connections as something noticed and picked up, not planted
- Group mind distinguished from its imitation, which is a room agreeing loudly
