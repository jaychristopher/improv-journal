One player is waiting at a bus stop and has a reason to stay there. Another arrives playing somebody specific. The waiting player's job is to be driven away — not to walk off, but to arrive at a genuine reason to leave — and once they do, the arriver takes the bench and somebody new comes on.

## What it trains

Entering as a whole person in one move. There is no scene to join and no offer to build on, so the arriving player has to be somebody the instant they step on, which makes this one of the cleanest tests of `initiation` in the shortform repertoire. A vague entrance produces nothing to react to and the round dies.

It also trains the half people neglect, which is the leaving. The exit has to be earned rather than announced, and finding the moment where a reasonable person would genuinely give up on that bench is a `justification` problem — you are constructing a reason backwards from a decision you already know you have to reach.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Escalating weirdness instead of committing.** The temptation is to be stranger rather than more specific, and strangeness has nowhere to go: once you are a shouting clown there is no second move. A precisely observed dull person clears the bench faster and can keep going.
- **Leaving too early.** The waiting player often bails on the first oddity, which throws the round away and teaches the arriver nothing about whether their choice was working.
- **Refusing to leave at all.** The opposite failure and the more annoying one, usually driven by not wanting to lose. There is nothing to win.

## The honest objection

The premise is that one player's goal is to end the interaction, which is structurally a blocking exercise wearing a game costume. Played badly it rehearses the instinct improv spends most of its time removing — treating your partner as an obstacle — and groups who run it often notice their scenes getting more adversarial for a fortnight afterwards.

The fix is in the framing. The arriving player is not trying to repel anybody; they are playing somebody fully, and the departure is a consequence rather than an objective. Coached that way it is a character exercise. Coached as a contest it is `performing-cleverness` with a bench.
