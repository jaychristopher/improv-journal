A hero is facing a crisis and calls for help. The player who enters is named by the one already there — Captain Mild Disappointment, The Human Tuesday — and has to become that person on the spot, then call for the next. Once the crisis is solved they leave in reverse order, each getting a final moment.

## What it trains

`endowment` in its most exposed form. You do not choose who you are; somebody else decides in the second before you arrive, and the only available move is to accept it completely and find out what it implies. There is no time to negotiate and no way to soften it.

The reverse exit is the part that is usually undervalued. Leaving in the opposite order forces `justification` retrospectively — the last hero out has to make sense of a crisis that four other people complicated, which is a genuine narrative skill and the reason the game is more than a naming contest.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Playing the name instead of a person.** Being called The Human Tuesday and then simply announcing Tuesday facts is a joke, not a character. The question is who somebody with that power actually is, and how it has shaped their life.
- **Names that are already the whole joke.** A name with nothing underneath it hands the next player nothing to build on. The generous ones suggest a personality rather than a punchline.
- **Solving the crisis too fast.** Each hero should make things somewhat worse. A game where the second arrival fixes everything has three players standing about.

## The honest objection

The naming is where the laughs are, so the game quietly rewards the person doing the naming rather than the person doing the playing — which inverts the ensemble ethic it appears to teach.

Watch a group play it twice and the names get longer and more elaborate while the characters get thinner, because everybody has worked out where the applause is. `performing-cleverness` has a specific home here and it looks like generosity. The fix is a coaching note rather than a rule change: name your partner in three words or fewer, and judge the round on what they did with it.
