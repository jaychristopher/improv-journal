[emphatic] Stop trying to be funny.

[short pause]

[contemplative] Every article on how to be funny tells you to DO something. Master your timing. Use callbacks. Self-deprecate. Observe like a comedian.

[emphatic] All of that is what funny people do. It's useless as instructions for becoming one.

[curious] Here's what 50 years of improv comedy reveals. Three moves that produce humor — none of which involve trying. Plus one thing you have to stop doing. All four in four minutes.

[emphatic] And every minute you spend trying — the real funny moment is happening, and you're not in it.

[short pause]

[teaching] There's a switch in your brain that explains why trying kills it.

[teaching] In 2008, neuroscientist Charles Limb put jazz musicians in an fMRI machine and watched what happened during spontaneous improvisation.

[emphatic] When the musicians improvised, the part of their brain that monitors performance — the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex — went quiet. The part that produces honest self-expression lit up.

[contemplative] Translation: spontaneous creativity requires the inner critic to switch off. Trying harder turns it back on.

[emphatic] Same idea as the overthinking video. Your attention is finite. When you spend it asking "what's the clever thing?" — you have nothing left for the actual moment in front of you. And the moment is where humor lives.

[short pause]

[teaching] Three moves. Then the antipattern.

[short pause]

[teaching] Move one. Honesty over jokes.

[emphatic] The funniest moment in any conversation is almost never the prepared joke. It's the moment someone says something unexpectedly true.

[curious] Listen to this. Quote — "I don't hate my job. I hate that my job requires me to pretend I read the emails."

[contemplative] That's not a joke. It's a specific truth, said without hedging. The humor comes from recognition. The listener thinks — yes, that's exactly right — and the surprise of hearing it out loud produces the laugh.

[short pause]

[emphatic] Keith Johnstone documented the paradox in 1979. Quote —

[short pause]

[emphatic] "The more obvious an improviser is, the more original he appears."

[contemplative] Your first instinct is connected to the real situation. Your second thought is connected to your ego trying to be impressive. The first is alive. The second is dead.

[short pause]

[teaching] Move two. Specificity over exaggeration.

[curious] "My boss is annoying" isn't funny.

[emphatic] "My boss starts every Zoom call by saying 'Can everyone see my screen?' and then waits thirty seconds for confirmation from each individual person." That's funny.

[contemplative] The detail creates the picture. The picture creates recognition. Specificity is the mechanism by which honesty becomes funny.

[teaching] Every other article tells you to exaggerate. Wrong. Be more specific. The precise detail reveals the absurdity that was already there.

[short pause]

[teaching] Move three. Pattern recognition over construction.

[curious] The Upright Citizens Brigade calls this — finding the game of the scene. Notice the first unusual thing. Then follow it.

[teaching] Your friend always apologizes for things that aren't their fault. That's the pattern. If they apologize for the weather — that's funny. If they apologize for being born — that's funnier.

[emphatic] You didn't construct a joke. You recognized a pattern and followed it.

[contemplative] You already use this skill at work. The only difference: at work you use pattern recognition to solve problems. In conversation you use it to illuminate them.

[short pause]

[teaching] Now the antipattern. The thing that kills all three moves.

[emphatic] Improvisers call it — performing cleverness. The person waiting for an opening to deliver their pre-composed zinger.

[contemplative] The zinger might be funny on its own. But it breaks the connection — because it wasn't a response to the moment. It was a performance inserted into the moment.

[teaching] Funny WITH someone builds connection. Funny AT someone — even an audience of one — interrupts it.

[short pause]

[warm] Three moves. Honesty. Specificity. Pattern recognition.

[warm] Plus one anti-move. Stop performing.

[emphatic] You don't become funny by adding a skill on top. You become funny when you stop the censor that was suppressing the real, specific, weird thing you already noticed.
