[curious] You know that feeling in a scene where you and your partner are clearly in the same place — without ever having explicitly said so? That's the thing I want to talk about.

[warm] Coherence.

[curious] Define it precisely. Because people confuse it with agreement.

[teaching] Coherence is the state where both players are tracking the same shared reality without having to negotiate it. Two characters can disagree violently and be perfectly coherent — they just occupy different positions within the same world. Coherence isn't about consensus. It's about whether you're building the same picture.

[short pause]

[curious] How do you feel it when it's working?

[matter-of-fact] The scene flows. Your partner's responses make sense. You don't have to guess what they think is happening. There's a felt sense of alignment — not because you discussed it, but because the signals have been clear enough that both of you built the same picture independently.

[contemplative] And when it's not working?

[warm] Confusion. Your offer lands wrong. Your partner reacts to something you didn't intend. You're in a boat, they're in a car. The audience senses something's off even if they can't name it. That's fracture — the breakdown of coherence.

[short pause]

[curious] So coherence isn't a switch — it's a spectrum?

[teaching] Spectrum, and it fluctuates beat to beat. You can be coherent on facts but incoherent on emotional register. You can drift apart slowly and re-cohere with a single clear offer. Maintenance is ongoing — you're constantly re-aligning, not holding a fixed state.

[curious] Then it isn't something you plan.

[matter-of-fact] You can't plan it. It emerges. From the accumulation of clearly sent and accurately received signals. Every accepted offer, every honest signal, every simple choice that downloads without friction — each one adds to the coherence of the shared state.

[short pause]

[contemplative] So it's a property of the connection, not of either performer.

[teaching] One player can be inventive, skilled, fully committed — and still incoherent with their partner. Coherence can't be a solo achievement. It's measured between players, never within one. This is why "I had a great scene" is sometimes wrong. You can have a great solo performance inside an incoherent scene. The scene is still failing.

[long pause]

[curious] The textbook reference here is Clark and Brennan, right?

[teaching] "Grounding in Communication," 1991. The interactive process of establishing mutual understanding through presentation, acceptance, and repair. Their "least collaborative effort" principle — simple, clear signals build coherence faster than complex ones. The simplest possible move that gets the job done wins.

[curious] Truth in Comedy phrased it differently. "Agreement is the one rule that can never be broken."

[matter-of-fact] Same insight. Different vocabulary. They're naming the substrate that everything else operates on top of.

[short pause]

[curious] What about absurdist improv? The whole point seems to be incoherence.

[warm] Even surreal comedy maintains rigorous internal logic once an absurd premise is established. Absurdism shifts the content of coherence — we agree the world is nonsensical — while maintaining the process. We're still tracking the same nonsense.

[contemplative] So coherence isn't about realism. It's about agreement on what the rules of this world are.

[teaching] Exactly. The world can be whatever. The agreement is everything.

[long pause]

[curious] How do you feel coherence fracturing in real time?

[matter-of-fact] You feel the pull to ask. "Wait, are we — ?" The body wants confirmation because the model has lost confidence. Before that — there's usually a beat where you reacted to a signal you didn't actually receive. You guessed. The guess was wrong. Now the pictures don't match.

[curious] And the move when you notice?

[teaching] Don't bulldoze. Don't pretend. Make a clear small offer — something with low ambiguity — and let your partner re-anchor. Or accept their next offer with extra clarity, slightly slower, slightly bigger. Re-establishing coherence isn't dramatic. It's a few seconds of slower, simpler signals.

[short pause]

[contemplative] This is why "be simple" matters so much.

[warm] Simple isn't dumb. Simple is the format that coherence can survive in. Every layer of complexity is another place coherence can fracture. The masters look easy because they've stopped fighting that — they let the signals stay simple so the connection stays clear.

[long pause]

[curious] Off stage?

[matter-of-fact] Every conversation has the same property. The good ones aren't the ones where you agree. They're the ones where you're tracking the same reality — even when you disagree within it. Every bad meeting, every misfired text, every fight that wasn't about what you thought it was about — coherence fractured somewhere and nobody noticed in time to repair.

[emphatic] The skill of noticing earlier is most of it. The repair, once you've noticed, is almost easy.
